## Important Mechanics

Pokemon is a pretty straight forward battling system, and to start brain storming how to turn it into a card game I'd like to look at a high level view of the system 
for some places to start.

1. Each player brings a team of monsters with the goal to knock out all the opposing monsters.

    Beating all your opponents creatures, or some number of creatures, or your opponent directly... or really any form of direct aggression and combat is a pretty
    basic and common end goal for a game.  I don't really see why our card game needs a different goal: beat your opponents team of monsters.

    Also important to note, we have a type of entity that we can use as a card type: *Monsters*.

    In the games, you can have one to three active Pokemon at a time, with the rest being on your bench.  You never gain new Pokemon during a battle.
    For our game, this will mean our monsters start on the field, and not in your deck.

    *There actually is a reason for a different goal, that being how games about building up towards one player winning have different multiplayer dynamics
    to games about knocking out opponents one at a time, but this game is being designed for one v one play mainly.*

2. Each monster has an elemental type or two associated with it and four moves that also have elemental types.

    First thing to note, we have to new types of things: elemental types and moves.  
    
    Color, element, class, etc are common ways of divying up and organizing mechanics in card games, so this isn't really something that needs to be created from scratch,
    we really just need a name for it to use in this system, I'm going to go with *Affinity*.

    Moves, however, are more interesting.  In the Pokemon TCG, moves are printed right on the Pokemon themselves, and different printings of the same Pokemon will have different moves.  This kind of makes sense and works similarly to how the video games do... however I don't think it's the right approach for this card game.
    One key aspect of the video games, and for card games, is customizability.  Pokemon in the games are basically a typed bag of stats with a wide list of available moves, meaning that two of the same Pokemon can be built to play entirely different roles.  Pokemon in the TCG are basically tools to use a move or ability.  
    
    Since I want to be able to change the moves a monster can use, we have our next card type: *Moves*.
